## What Is a Mortgage Rate Lock?

A rate lock is a lender's commitment to hold your interest rate, and any points, for a set number of days. If rates rise before closing, your rate stays the same. If rates fall, a standard lock usually keeps your original rate unless you have a float-down option.

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/whats-a-lock-in-or-a-rate-lock-en-143/) notes that a lock depends on your loan closing within the lock period. Changes to your application, such as the loan amount, credit score, or appraised value, can still change the rate.

## Should I Lock Now or Float?

Floating means waiting to lock in hopes of a lower rate. It can pay off, but it's a bet. Rates respond to inflation, jobs data, bond markets, and the Federal Reserve. For background, read [what moves mortgage rates](/posts/what-moves-mortgage-rates/).

The Fed held its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% on [October 28](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm). Two voting members preferred a quarter-point increase, and the statement said inflation "remains elevated." That split is a reason for caution if you plan to float. We covered that meeting in our [October Fed recap](/posts/fed-meeting-october-2026-mortgage-rates/).

A simple rule of thumb:

- **Lock** if today's payment fits your budget and a rate increase would hurt your approval or your comfort.
- **Consider floating** only if you can absorb a higher rate and have time before closing.

What does a small move cost? On a $400,000 30-year loan, principal and interest is about $2,734 at 7.27%. At 7.52% it's about $2,802, or roughly $68 more each month. These figures are illustrations, not quotes.

## How Long Should a Mortgage Rate Lock Be?

Choose a lock that covers your contract closing date with some room to spare:

- **30 days:** Common for a refinance or a quick purchase with a clean file.
- **45 days:** A safer choice for many purchases, especially around holidays.
- **60 days or more:** Useful for new construction or complex files, usually at a higher cost.

This year, Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26, and late December is a busy time for closings. Title companies, appraisers, and underwriters often work shorter weeks around the holidays. ## What Are Float-Downs and Lock Extensions?

- **Float-down option:** Some lenders let you lower your locked rate once if rates fall by a set amount. It may carry a fee or a slightly higher starting price.
- **Lock extension:** If closing slips past your lock date, the lender may charge to extend. Ask about the cost per day or per week up front.
- **Relock:** If a lock expires, you may have to relock at the current market rate, or the worse of the old and new rates, depending on the lender.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a mortgage rate lock last?

Most rate locks last 30, 45, or 60 days. Longer locks are available, usually at a higher cost. Choose a lock that covers your expected closing date plus a buffer for delays.

### Does it cost money to lock a mortgage rate?

Standard locks of 30 to 60 days are often included in the rate pricing. Longer locks, float-down options, and lock extensions may cost extra. Ask your lender to explain any lock fees in writing.

### What happens if rates drop after I lock?

With a standard lock, you usually keep your locked rate. Some lenders offer a float-down option that lets you capture a lower rate once, often for a fee or a slightly higher starting price.

### Can I lock my rate before I find a house?

Most lenders lock a rate for a specific property and loan. Some offer lock-and-shop programs that lock before you find a home, usually for a fee or a slightly higher rate.

### Will the December Fed meeting change mortgage rates?

The Fed sets short-term rates, not mortgage rates, but its decisions and outlook can move the bond market. Mortgage rates often react before and after Fed meetings. No one can predict the direction with certainty.
