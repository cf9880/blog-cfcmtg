## What Do Realtors Need From a Mortgage Lender?

A real estate agent's job is to get the client to the closing table. The loan side of that job usually comes down to five practical needs:

1. **A clear, written preapproval** the listing agent can trust.
2. **Fast answers** when an offer is being written or a counteroffer comes in.
3. **Honest updates** if a condition, appraisal, or title issue appears.
4. **Knowledge of the right loan program**, including VA, FHA, USDA, jumbo, and investor loans when those fit the buyer.
5. **Availability outside a 9-to-5 weekday window**, because many showings, negotiations, and inspection calls happen at night or on weekends.

Those points are about process, not slogans. A missed call on a Saturday can cost a buyer a home. A vague preapproval can cause a seller to take another offer.

## Why Does a Mortgage Broker Matter to Agents?

Banks and credit unions generally offer their own loan products. A **mortgage broker** works with multiple wholesale lenders and helps the borrower compare programs, fees, and guidelines. Our post on [mortgage broker vs. bank](/posts/mortgage-broker-vs-bank/) walks through the difference in more detail.

For a Realtor, that structure can matter when:

- The buyer is self-employed and needs a flexible income review.
- A first-time buyer may fit FHA or a conventional loan with a smaller down payment.
- A veteran wants to use a [VA home loan](/posts/va-home-loan-guide-veterans-day/).
- The purchase is above the conforming loan limit and needs a jumbo program.
- The client is buying a rental and may qualify with a [DSCR loan](/posts/dscr-loan-guide/).

The agent does not have to guess which box the buyer fits. The loan officer can compare options and put a program on the preapproval that matches the property and the borrower's file.

## How Does a Strong Preapproval Help an Offer?

Sellers and listing agents want to know the buyer can close. A useful preapproval typically includes:

- The borrower's name and the loan officer's contact information
- A maximum purchase price or loan amount based on a credit and income review
- The likely loan type (for example, conventional or FHA)
- Any major conditions, such as the sale of another home

It is not a commitment to lend. Underwriting still has to review the contract, the appraisal, and updated documents. But a letter that was issued after a real review is more useful in a multiple-offer situation than a casual prequalification.

Once the buyer is under contract, speed still matters. [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/learn/how-long-does-it-take-to-close-on-a-house/), citing a 2026 Intercontinental Exchange study, reports that the average time to close a home purchase is about 37 days, and that conventional loans often close 30 to 45 days after the contract is signed. Agents who send complete documents quickly, and who work with a lender who answers conditions quickly, give that timeline a better chance.

## What Loan Programs Can Agents Expect Their Buyers to Be Considered For?

Depending on the property and the borrower's situation, buyers may be a fit for:

- **Conventional** loans for many primary-residence purchases and refinances
- **FHA** loans for buyers who need a lower down payment or more flexible credit guidelines
- **VA** loans for eligible veterans, service members, and some surviving spouses
- **USDA** loans in eligible rural and some suburban areas
- **Jumbo** loans above the conforming limit
- **HELOCs** and cash-out refinances for homeowners who want to use equity
- **DSCR** loans for investment properties, which typically qualify on rental income rather than personal debt-to-income

Program rules vary by lender and are not a guarantee of approval. The value for the agent is having one point of contact who can compare those options instead of sending the buyer to start over with a new lender.
