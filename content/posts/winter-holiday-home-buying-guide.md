**Yes, buying a home in winter can work in your favor. Fewer buyers shop between Thanksgiving and New Year's, and homes sold more slowly last winter: the National Association of REALTORS® (NAR) reported a median of 39 days on market in December 2025, up from 35 a year earlier. Slower sales usually mean more room to negotiate.**

Here's what the national numbers say, how today's mortgage rates fit in, and how to close before the year ends without missing the holidays.

## Key Takeaways

- **Less competition:** NAR's median days on market rose to **39 in December 2025**, from 35 in December 2024 ([NAR REALTORS® Confidence Index](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2026-01/2025-12-realtors-confidence-index-01-21-2026.pdf)).
- **Prices held steady:** The national median existing-home price was **$405,400** in December 2025, up just 0.4% from a year earlier.
- **More homes to choose from now:** U.S. inventory hit **1.60 million** homes in September 2026, a **4.8-month supply**.
- **Rates today:** Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed index was **7.33%** on Oct. 26, 2026, near its lowest level in more than a month.
- **Start now:** A preapproval before Thanksgiving makes a December closing far more realistic.

## Is Winter a Good Time to Buy a House?

For many buyers, yes. Freddie Mac notes that winter usually brings **fewer buyers**, and that holiday-season sellers are often **motivated to negotiate** on price, closing costs, closing date, or items like appliances ([Freddie Mac My Home](https://myhome.freddiemac.com/blog/homebuying/buy-or-sell-in-winter)).

Last winter's national data backs that up:

| U.S. existing-home market | Dec. 2024 | Dec. 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Median existing-home price | $403,700 | $405,400 |
| Median days on market | 35 | 39 |
| Months of supply | 3.2 | 3.3 |

*Sources: [NAR Existing-Home Sales, December 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/01/14/3218834/0/en/NAR-Existing-Home-Sales-Report-Shows-5-1-Increase-in-December.html) and NAR REALTORS® Confidence Index.*

Homes still sold, and prices still edged up. But they sat longer, and that gives a prepared buyer leverage.

Two more signs pointed the same way:

- **Sellers outnumbered buyers.** Redfin estimated that U.S. home sellers outnumbered buyers by **45% in December 2025**, the largest gap in its records going back to 2013 ([Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/news/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Buyers-vs-Sellers-Release-Jan-2026.pdf)).
- **Price cuts stayed common.** Realtor.com found **12.9%** of listings had a price cut in December 2025, and the median list price was **$399,950**, down 3.6% from November ([Realtor.com](https://www.realtor.com/research/december-2025-data/)).

**The trade-off:** fewer listings. Realtor.com's active listings fell 8.9% from November to December 2025, which is normal for the season. You may see fewer homes, but you may also face fewer rival offers.

## What Is the Housing Market Doing Heading Into Winter 2026?

NAR's latest report (released Oct. 13, 2026) shows a market with more choice than buyers have had in years ([NAR](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/existing-home-sales-increased-1-5-in-september-according-to-nar-report)):

- **Sales pace:** 3.99 million (seasonally adjusted annual rate), down 2.2% from a year earlier
- **Median price:** $417,500, up 1.3% year over year
- **Inventory:** 1.60 million homes, a 4.8-month supply, up from 4.5 months a year earlier

Realtor.com called that the highest inventory level since November 2019. NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun said August and September were the two slowest months for sales this year, as higher rates meant fewer buyers could qualify. For a buyer who *can* qualify, that is the setup for a calmer winter search.

## What Can You Negotiate When Buying a Home Over the Holidays?

When a home has been listed for a while, sellers are often more flexible. Ask your agent about:

1. **Seller-paid closing costs** to lower your cash to close
2. **A temporary or permanent rate buydown**, which can soften a 7%-plus rate
3. **Repairs or a repair credit** after inspection
4. **A closing date that fits you**, such as early January
5. **Appliances or furnishings** left with the home

Results vary by home and seller. Loan programs also limit how much a seller can contribute, so ask your loan officer before you write the offer.

## How Do You Close on a House Before the End of the Year?

NAR reported a **median of 30 days to close** in December 2025. Working backward, that means getting under contract by about **late November** for a December closing. To keep it on track:

- **Get preapproved now.** Have pay stubs, W-2s, two months of bank statements, and ID ready.
- **Avoid new credit.** Hold off on holiday financing for furniture, electronics, or a new car until after closing.
- **Plan around closures.** Thanksgiving is Nov. 26, Christmas falls on Friday, Dec. 25, and New Year's Day is Friday, Jan. 1, 2027. Lenders, title companies, and county recording offices may close or run short-staffed.
- **Allow for the Closing Disclosure.** You must receive it at least **three business days** before closing ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-closing-disclosure-en-1983/)), so holidays can push your date.
- **Respond fast.** Answer underwriting requests the same day when you can.

**A tax note:** If you itemize, mortgage interest, property taxes, and certain points paid on a main home may be deductible for the year you pay them ([IRS Publication 936](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936)). Rules and limits apply, so talk with a tax professional before you count on it.

## What Should You Check When Touring Homes in Winter?

Winter showings can reveal things a sunny June tour hides:

- **Heating:** Is the house warm? How old is the furnace or heat pump?
- **Drafts and windows:** Feel for cold spots near windows and doors.
- **Roof and gutters:** Look for ice buildup, sagging gutters, or water stains on ceilings.
- **Drainage:** Watch where rain or snowmelt goes. Standing water near the foundation is a red flag.
- **Utility bills:** Ask the seller for recent winter bills.

Then book your inspection quickly. Inspectors' calendars fill up around the holidays too.
