**Mortgage rates are a little higher heading into Thanksgiving week 2026. Freddie Mac's 30-year fixed averaged 7.28% on November 19, up from 7.11% the week before, and Mortgage News Daily's daily index sat at 7.34% on November 23. Sales are slow, which gives prepared buyers more room to negotiate.**

Thanksgiving is a week for family, food, and football, but plenty of people are also watching mortgage rates. If you're buying a home this winter or thinking about tapping your equity, here's a plain-English look at where mortgage rates stand this Thanksgiving week, what pushed them up, and the dates that could move them before the end of the year.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rates rose for a second week.** Freddie Mac's 30-year average hit 7.28% on Nov. 19, 2026, versus 6.26% a year earlier.
- **Daily rates were flat to start the week.** Mortgage News Daily's 30-year index was 7.34% on Nov. 23, up just 0.01%.
- **Home sales are at multi-year lows.** NAR said October existing-home sales fell to a 3.75 million annual pace, the lowest since August 2010.
- **Inventory is improving.** There were 1.59 million homes for sale in October, a 5.1-month supply.
- **Watch two dates:** Freddie Mac's early release on Wed., Nov. 25, and the Fed meeting on Dec. 8-9.

### What does a 0.17% rate jump cost?

On a $300,000 loan, principal and interest at 7.11% is about $2,018 a month. At 7.28%, it is about $2,053, or roughly $35 more per month. That is real money, but it is not a reason to panic. Small weekly moves often reverse. *Example payments are illustrative, not quotes, and exclude taxes and insurance.*

## Why Did Mortgage Rates Rise Before Thanksgiving?

Mortgage rates track the bond market, especially mortgage-backed securities (MBS). An **MBS** is a bond made from a pool of home loans. When investors pay less for MBS, lenders raise rates.

- **Bond yields rose in mid-November.** The MBA said its 30-year average climbed to 7.35%, its highest in three weeks, and total applications fell 2.3% for the week ending November 13.
- **Market news moves rates daily.** On November 23, Mortgage News Daily reported a volatile morning tied to big stock-market news, but most lenders' rates ended the day close to unchanged.
- **The Fed is in focus.** The Federal Reserve's next meeting is December 8-9, 2026. The Fed sets short-term rates, not mortgage rates, but its signals can push bond yields up or down.

For a deeper look at the forces behind rate moves, see our guide on [what moves mortgage rates](/posts/what-moves-mortgage-rates/).

## Is Thanksgiving Week a Good Time to Buy a Home?

For many buyers, yes. The housing market is quiet, and quiet markets often favor buyers who are ready.

- **Fewer competing buyers.** NAR's Pending Home Sales Index fell 4.7% in October, the second straight record low for contract signings.
- **More homes to choose from.** Inventory was up 4.6% from a year earlier in October, and the median home took 35 days to sell, up from 33 in September.
- **Prices are barely rising.** The national median existing-home price was $416,700 in October, up just 0.4% from a year earlier.

That mix can create room to ask sellers for help with closing costs or a rate buydown. A **rate buydown** is money paid up front, often by the seller, to lower your interest rate for part or all of the loan.

## What Should Buyers Do Before the December Fed Meeting?

1. **Get preapproved now.** Lenders and appraisers slow down around the holidays, so start your file before December.
2. **Plan your lock.** If you're under contract, read our [November rate lock guide](/posts/mortgage-rate-lock-guide-november-2026/) to choose a lock length that covers your closing date.
3. **Watch your holiday spending.** New credit card balances or a new car loan can change your debt-to-income ratio and your approval.
4. **Know the loan limits.** If your loan is near $832,750, check our update on [2027 conforming loan limits](/posts/2027-conforming-loan-limits/), which FHFA usually announces in late November.
5. **Compare loan types.** FHA and VA averages were lower than conventional on Nov. 23, so ask whether you qualify.

## What If You Already Own a Home?

With 30-year rates above 7%, most owners with older, lower rates won't benefit from a standard refinance. If you want cash for projects, debt payoff, or college costs, a **HELOC** (home equity line of credit) lets you borrow against your equity without replacing your first mortgage.
