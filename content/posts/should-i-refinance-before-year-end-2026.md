**Should you refinance before year-end 2026? For many homeowners, a simple rate-and-term refinance won't pay off right now, because today's 7.18% average is above where many loans were made. But a refinance can still help if your rate is near 7.5% or higher, if you want to drop FHA mortgage insurance or PMI, or if an ARM is about to reset.**

Mortgage rates have eased for three straight weeks, and the holidays are a natural time to review your household budget. Here's a clear way to decide whether a refinance belongs on your year-end to-do list.

## Key Takeaways

- **Rates are easing but still high.** Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 7.18% on November 5, 2026, down from 7.40% in early October, but up from 6.22% a year earlier.
- **Rate-and-term refinances work best for higher-rate loans.** Freddie Mac's weekly average peaked at 7.79% in October 2023, and 2024's high was 7.22%.
- **There are other reasons to refinance:** removing FHA mortgage insurance, ending PMI, fixing an ARM, removing a co-borrower, or changing your term.
- **Know your break-even point.** Divide closing costs by monthly savings.
- **Watch December 8-9.** The Fed's next meeting could move markets.

## Who Might Benefit From Refinancing Now?

### 1. Homeowners with a rate near 7.5% or higher

Freddie Mac's data shows the 30-year average topped 7.5% in parts of late 2023, with a peak of 7.79% on October 26, 2023. If you locked near that peak, today's rates may already offer savings.

### 2. FHA borrowers who want to drop mortgage insurance

FHA loans with less than 10% down generally carry monthly mortgage insurance for the life of the loan. If your home has gained value and you now have about 20% equity, a conventional refinance can remove it, sometimes even at a similar rate.

### 3. Conventional borrowers paying PMI

PMI on conventional loans can often be removed once you reach enough equity, sometimes without a refinance. Ask your servicer first. If your loan is otherwise due for a change, a refinance can do both.

### 4. ARM borrowers facing a reset

If your adjustable-rate mortgage is about to adjust, a fixed-rate refinance can make your payment predictable.

### 5. Life changes

Divorce, removing a co-borrower, or switching from a 30-year to a 15-year loan are common reasons to refinance, even when the rate savings are small.

### 6. VA and FHA streamline options

If you already have a VA or FHA loan at a higher rate, a VA Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan (IRRRL) or FHA Streamline may offer a lighter-documentation path. The VA lists a 0.5% funding fee for an IRRRL.

## How Do You Figure Your Break-Even Point?

**Break-even** is how long it takes for your monthly savings to cover your closing costs.

**Example:** You owe $350,000 at 7.79% on a 30-year loan. Your principal and interest are about $2,517 per month. At 7.18%, a new 30-year loan on the same balance would be about $2,371. That saves about $146 a month. If closing costs were $5,000, your break-even would be about 34 months.

If you plan to stay longer than that, the refinance may make sense. If you might move sooner, it may not. Remember that restarting a 30-year term can raise your total interest even when the monthly payment drops. These are illustrations, not quotes.

## What About Cash-Out Refinancing?

If you mainly need cash for a project or to pay off debt, compare a cash-out refinance with a HELOC. If your current rate is well below 7%, a HELOC usually lets you keep it. Bankrate's national average HELOC rate was 7.49% on November 4, 2026. Our [HELOC vs. cash-out refinance guide](/posts/heloc-vs-cash-out-refinance-2026/) walks through the math.

## Is There a Tax Angle to Refinancing Before Year-End?

Points you pay on a refinance are usually deducted over the life of the loan rather than all at once, according to IRS guidance. Rules differ for cash used to improve your home. Our [mortgage interest deduction checklist](/posts/mortgage-interest-deduction-2026-taxes/) covers the basics. Talk with a tax professional before you decide.

## How Do You Prepare for a Year-End Refinance?

1. **Pull your current statement** and note your rate, balance, and any mortgage insurance.
2. **Estimate your home's value** to see your equity.
3. **Check your credit** and avoid new debt before you apply.
4. **Gather documents:** pay stubs, W-2s, tax returns, bank statements, and homeowners insurance.
5. **Plan around the holidays.** Appraisers, title companies, and underwriters often have shorter schedules in late December.
6. **Know your lock options.** Our [rate lock guide](/posts/mortgage-rate-lock-guide-november-2026/) explains lock length and float-downs.
