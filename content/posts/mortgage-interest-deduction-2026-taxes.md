**The mortgage interest deduction in 2026 still lets homeowners who itemize deduct interest on up to $750,000 of home loan debt. Two things are new for 2026: mortgage insurance premiums are deductible again for many households, and the state and local tax (SALT) cap rises to $40,400. Whether you benefit depends on beating a $32,200 standard deduction for married couples.**

With about eight weeks left in the year, now is a good time to check whether your mortgage will lower your 2026 tax bill. This guide covers what changed, who benefits, and a simple year-end checklist. It's general information, not tax advice, so confirm your situation with a CPA or tax preparer.

## Key Takeaways

- **The $750,000 limit is now permanent.** The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) made the post-2017 mortgage interest rules permanent, according to the IRS.
- **Mortgage insurance is deductible again for 2026,** including PMI and FHA mortgage insurance premiums, with an income phase-out starting at $100,000 of adjusted gross income (AGI).
- **The SALT cap is $40,400 for 2026** ($20,200 if married filing separately), which can make itemizing worthwhile for more homeowners.
- **The 2026 standard deduction** is $32,200 for married couples filing jointly, $16,100 for single filers, and $24,150 for heads of household.
- **Home equity interest counts only** if the money was used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home that secures the loan.

## How Does the Mortgage Interest Deduction Work in 2026?

You can deduct qualified mortgage interest only if you itemize on Schedule A. Per the IRS ([Publication 936](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936)), the limit is interest on up to **$750,000** of home acquisition debt ($375,000 if married filing separately). Loans taken out before December 16, 2017, keep the older $1 million limit.

The debt can be on your main home and one second home. Your lender reports the interest you paid on **Form 1098**, which usually arrives by the end of January.

**How much interest is that?** On a $400,000, 30-year fixed loan at 7.22% (Mortgage News Daily's average on November 4, 2026), the first year's interest is about **$28,750**. That's an illustrative example, not a quote. Interest is front-loaded, so newer loans produce the largest deductions.

## Is PMI Tax Deductible in 2026?

Yes, for many borrowers. Under the 2025 law, mortgage insurance premiums are treated as deductible mortgage interest again starting in tax year 2026. The IRS draft 2026 Schedule A adds a line for them. Qualifying premiums include:

- Private mortgage insurance (PMI) on conventional loans
- FHA mortgage insurance premiums
- The VA funding fee
- The USDA (Rural Housing Service) guarantee fee

The deduction phases out between **$100,000 and $109,000 of AGI** ($50,000 to $54,500 if married filing separately). Prepaid mortgage insurance is generally spread over 84 months or the life of the loan, whichever is shorter. If you financed a VA or FHA loan recently, ask your tax preparer how this applies to you.

## Will You Itemize or Take the Standard Deduction?

The deduction only helps if your itemized total beats the standard deduction. For 2026, those amounts are:

| Filing status | 2026 standard deduction |
|---|---|
| Married filing jointly | $32,200 |
| Head of household | $24,150 |
| Single or married filing separately | $16,100 |

*Source: IRS, Revenue Procedure 2025-32.*

Itemized deductions usually come from mortgage interest, mortgage insurance, state and local taxes, and charitable gifts. The higher SALT cap matters here. For 2026, you can deduct up to **$40,400** of state and local income or sales taxes plus property taxes. The cap shrinks for incomes above $505,000 but not below $10,000.

**Example:** A married couple pays $28,000 in mortgage interest, $6,500 in property and state taxes, and gives $2,000 to charity. Their $36,500 total beats the $32,200 standard deduction, so itemizing saves them tax on the $4,300 difference. Homeowners in states without an income tax, like Florida and Tennessee, may have smaller SALT totals, so the math can go either way.

## Does HELOC Interest Count?

Only in some cases. HELOC or home equity loan interest is deductible when you use the money to **buy, build, or substantially improve** the home that secures the loan. Interest on money used for credit cards, cars, or tuition doesn't qualify. The combined balance of all qualifying loans still counts toward the $750,000 limit.

## What Should Homeowners Do Before December 31?

1. **Look up your year-to-date interest** in your lender's online portal so you're not surprised in January.
2. **Ask whether a January payment counts.** Interest is generally deductible in the year you pay it. Paying January's bill in December may add one month of interest to 2026. Check with your tax preparer first.
3. **Gather closing documents** if you bought or refinanced in 2026. Points you paid on a purchase may be deductible, and the closing disclosure shows them.
4. **Total your property taxes.** Count only taxes actually paid in 2026.
5. **Track home improvements.** Receipts can matter for HELOC interest and for your cost basis when you sell.
6. **Review your rate.** Rates moved a lot this year. Freddie Mac's 30-year average was 7.24% on October 29, 2026, up from 6.17% a year earlier. If you're deciding when to act, our [rate lock guide](/posts/mortgage-rate-lock-guide-november-2026/) and [what moves mortgage rates](/posts/what-moves-mortgage-rates/) can help.

## Should Taxes Drive Your Mortgage Decision?

Not on their own. A tax deduction lowers your cost, but you still pay the interest. Choose your loan based on the payment, the total cost, and your plans, then treat any tax savings as a bonus.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is mortgage interest still tax deductible in 2026?

Yes. If you itemize, you can deduct interest on up to $750,000 of home acquisition debt, or $375,000 if married filing separately. Loans from before December 16, 2017, keep the $1 million limit. The 2025 tax law made these limits permanent.

### Can I deduct PMI on my 2026 taxes?

Yes, for many borrowers. Starting in 2026, PMI, FHA mortgage insurance, the VA funding fee and the USDA guarantee fee count as deductible mortgage interest. The deduction phases out between $100,000 and $109,000 of adjusted gross income.

### What is the 2026 standard deduction?

For 2026, the standard deduction is $32,200 for married couples filing jointly, $24,150 for heads of household, and $16,100 for single filers and married couples filing separately, according to the IRS.

### What is the SALT cap for 2026?

The 2026 cap on state and local tax deductions is $40,400, or $20,200 if married filing separately. It is reduced for modified adjusted gross income above $505,000, but not below $10,000.

### Is HELOC interest tax deductible?

Only when the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures the HELOC. Interest on HELOC money used for other expenses, like debt payoff or a car, is not deductible.
