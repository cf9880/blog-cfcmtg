**Yes, holiday spending can affect your mortgage approval. New credit cards, store financing, and higher card balances can lower your credit score and raise your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio, and lenders often re-check credit before closing. If you're buying or refinancing this winter, keep new debt off your credit report until after you close.**

## Key Takeaways

- **Don't open new credit** (store cards, "buy now, pay later," car loans) while you're applying for a mortgage.
- **Keep card balances low.** Higher balances can lower your score and raise your minimum payments.
- **Your DTI ratio matters.** For loans run through Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter, the maximum DTI is 50%, but lower is safer.
- **Cash gifts for a down payment need a paper trail,** usually a signed gift letter and proof of transfer.
- **Rates moved higher this week.** Mortgage News Daily's 30-year fixed index was 7.30% on November 10, 2026, after a hotter-than-expected inflation report.

## How Does Holiday Spending Affect Mortgage Approval?

Lenders look at three big things: your credit, your income, and your debts. Holiday spending can touch all three.

- **Credit score:** Opening a new account adds a hard inquiry and a new tradeline. Running up existing cards raises your **credit utilization**, which is the share of your available credit you're using. Both can lower your score.
- **Monthly debts:** A new card balance, store financing plan, or loan adds a required monthly payment. That payment counts against you in underwriting.
- **Cash to close:** Money spent on gifts and travel is money that isn't in the bank for your down payment, closing costs, and reserves.

## What Is a Debt-to-Income Ratio?

Your **debt-to-income ratio (DTI)** is your total monthly debt payments, including your new house payment, divided by your gross monthly income.

According to [Fannie Mae's Selling Guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-6-02/debt-income-ratios), loans underwritten with its Desktop Underwriter system can go up to a 50% DTI. Manually underwritten loans are generally capped at 36%, or up to 45% with strong compensating factors. FHA, VA, and other programs have their own rules.

Here's a simple example. It's an illustration only, not a quote or approval:

| Item | Before the holidays | After a new store card |
|---|---|---|
| Gross monthly income | $8,000 | $8,000 |
| New house payment (all-in) | $2,900 | $2,900 |
| Car and student loan payments | $650 | $650 |
| New store card minimum payment | $0 | $150 |
| **DTI** | **44.4%** | **46.3%** |

A $150 minimum payment might not feel like much. But if you were close to a program's limit, it could shrink the loan amount you qualify for, or change your loan terms.

## Can Lenders See New Debt After I'm Preapproved?

Often, yes. A preapproval is based on a snapshot of your credit. Many lenders re-check credit or monitor for new accounts before closing, and you'll typically be asked to explain recent inquiries. If a new debt shows up, the lender may need to recalculate your DTI. That can delay closing or change your approval.

The safest plan: **talk to your loan officer before you open, close, or co-sign on anything.**

## Can I Use Holiday Gift Money for a Down Payment?

Usually, yes. Most loan programs allow gift funds from eligible donors, such as relatives. Expect to provide:

1. **A signed gift letter** stating the amount, the donor's relationship to you, and that no repayment is expected.
2. **Proof of the transfer,** such as bank statements showing the money leaving the donor's account and arriving in yours.
3. **Clean records.** Large cash deposits with no paper trail are hard to use. A check or bank transfer is easier to document than cash in a card.

Rules on who can give and how much you must contribute yourself vary by loan type, so ask before the money moves.

## What Should I Avoid Until After Closing?

- Opening new credit cards, even for a one-time store discount
- Financing furniture, appliances, or a car
- Using "buy now, pay later" plans for big purchases
- Moving large sums between accounts without records
- Changing jobs or switching from salary to commission
- Closing old credit cards (it can raise your utilization)
- Missing any payment, even a small one, during busy travel weeks

## How Can I Enjoy the Holidays and Still Buy a Home?

- **Set a cash budget** for gifts and travel, and stick to it.
- **Use cards you already have,** and pay them down before the statement date if you can.
- **Keep copies** of bank statements, pay stubs, and any gift letters in one folder.
- **Shop for a home now.** Fewer buyers tend to look between Thanksgiving and New Year's. Our [winter holiday home buying guide](/posts/winter-holiday-home-buying-guide/) covers timing tips.
