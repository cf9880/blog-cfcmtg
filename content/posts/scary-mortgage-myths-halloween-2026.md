Halloween is the one time of year when a little fright is fun. Buying a home shouldn't be scary, though. Plenty of would-be buyers get spooked by myths that just don't hold up. Below, we bust five of the most common "scary" mortgage myths, share where rates stand this October, and lay out the tricks and treats of buying a home this fall.

## Where Mortgage Rates Stand in Mid-October 2026

- **Freddie Mac PMMS:** The 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged **7.40%** as of October 15, 2026, unchanged from the prior week. A year earlier it averaged 6.27%. The 15-year fixed averaged **6.71%**, down from 6.73% the prior week. ([Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms))
- **Mortgage News Daily:** The daily index for a top-tier 30-year fixed was **7.37%** on October 16, 2026, after a sharp drop over the previous week brought rates to roughly three-week lows. On the same day, MND's index showed 30-year FHA at 6.99% and 30-year VA at 7.01%. ([Mortgage News Daily](https://www.mortgagenewsdaily.com/))

**The takeaway:** Rates are higher than they were a year ago, and they've been moving around a lot from day to day. That's exactly why your loan program, credit profile, and timing matter. A licensed loan officer can show you real options for your own situation. Averages are just averages.

## Myth #1: "You Need 20% Down to Buy a Home"

**Busted.** A 20% down payment lets you skip mortgage insurance on a conventional loan, but it isn't required. Some conventional programs allow as little as 3% down for qualified buyers. FHA loans allow 3.5% down with a qualifying credit score. Eligible veterans and service members may qualify for VA loans with no down payment, and USDA loans offer zero-down financing in eligible areas.

## Myth #2: "Your Credit Has to Be Perfect"

**Busted.** Good credit helps you get better pricing, but government-backed programs like FHA are built to be more flexible. A loan officer can also suggest steps that may improve your score before you apply.

## Myth #3: "Prequalifying Will Wreck Your Credit"

**Mostly myth.** A preapproval usually includes a credit check, which may cause a small, temporary dip in your score. Credit scoring models generally count multiple mortgage inquiries within a short shopping window as a single inquiry. So comparing your options won't haunt you.

## Myth #4: "Rates Are High, So I Should Wait"

**It depends.** Nobody can predict where rates will go, and they've moved sharply in both directions this fall. Waiting could pay off. It could also mean higher prices or more competition later. Make the decision with real numbers for your budget and goals, not headlines alone.

## Myth #5: "Investment Property Loans Require Tax Returns Showing Big Income"

**Not always.** **DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) loans** are mainly qualified on the property's rental income compared with its housing payment, not your personal income. That makes them popular with real estate investors and self-employed borrowers.

## Tricks and Treats of Buying a Home This Fall

**Treats 🍬**
- Fall often brings less competition than the spring rush.
- Sellers whose homes have sat on the market may be more open to negotiating.
- You could be settled in before the holidays.

**Tricks 🎃 (to avoid)**
- Opening new credit cards or financing a car while your loan is in process.
- Skipping the home inspection. Some haunted surprises hide behind the walls!
- Shopping for homes before you know your budget. Get preapproved first.

## A Little Haunted-House Trivia

- Jack-o'-lanterns started as carved turnips in Ireland and Scotland. Immigrants to America switched to pumpkins, which were bigger and easier to carve.
- In some states, courts have weighed whether a seller should have disclosed a home's "haunted" reputation. Disclosure rules for these so-called "stigmatized properties" vary by state. Always read your disclosures and ask questions!
