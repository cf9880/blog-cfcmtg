**Yes, there is still time to close on a home before year-end 2026 if you get preapproved now and go under contract soon with a realistic late-December closing date. A 2026 Intercontinental Exchange study cited by Zillow found that the average purchase closing takes about 37 days, and conventional loans often close 30 to 45 days after the contract is signed. Closing by Thanksgiving is generally not realistic unless you are already under contract.**

Today is mid-November. The holidays are close, but a typical mortgage timeline can still fit before December 31 if you start promptly and leave room for holiday office closures. This is a planning guide, not a promise that any specific date will work for your file.

## Key Takeaways

- **The average closing is about 37 days**, according to a 2026 ICE study cited by [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/learn/how-long-does-it-take-to-close-on-a-house/). Plan on 30 to 45 days from an accepted contract.
- **Thanksgiving (November 26) is likely too soon** to start a new purchase. Late December or year-end is the more realistic target.
- **The Closing Disclosure must arrive at least three business days before closing**, per the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/when-do-i-get-a-closing-disclosure-en-179/).
- **Winter buyers often face less competition.** The National Association of REALTORS® reported a 5.1-month supply of existing homes in October 2026, up from 4.4 months a year earlier.
- **Rates eased this week.** Freddie Mac's 30-year average was **7.11%** on November 12, 2026, the lowest level in seven weeks.

## How Long Does a Mortgage Closing Really Take?

After you and the seller sign a contract, the clock starts on inspections, the appraisal, title work, underwriting, and the final Closing Disclosure. Zillow reports that conventional financing commonly takes **30 to 45 days** from that signed contract, with an average of about **37 days** in a 2026 ICE study.

Counted from mid-November:

- **Thanksgiving, November 26:** about 11 days from today. That is shorter than a typical mortgage closing.
- **Christmas, December 25:** about 40 days. That can fit a 30- to 45-day contract, but holiday closures make it tight.
- **December 31:** about 46 days. That is a more comfortable window for a standard close, as long as documents and third parties stay on schedule.

If you are already under contract, you may still make an earlier date. If you have not started touring homes or getting preapproved, treat year-end as the goal, not Thanksgiving dinner in the new dining room.

Cash buyers can sometimes close faster, often in one to two weeks, because they do not wait on a loan. Most buyers do use financing, so plan around the mortgage timeline.

## What Legal Waiting Periods Cannot Be Shortened?

Two timing rules show up on almost every mortgage:

1. The lender generally must give you a **Loan Estimate** within three business days of a full application.
2. You must receive the **Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing**.

The CFPB explains the Closing Disclosure rule in plain language: you get the form at least three business days before you sign. If the APR, the loan product, or a prepayment penalty changes in a way that requires a new disclosure, a new three-business-day wait can start. Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day are federal holidays, so they do not count as business days for that waiting period. If you want to close on Wednesday, December 23, your disclosure needs to be in your hands early enough to clear those three days.

That is why a "close the day before Christmas" plan can slip if the disclosure goes out late.

## Will the Holidays Slow Things Down?

They can. Many appraisers, title offices, insurance agents, and HOA contacts take time off around Thanksgiving and Christmas. Wiring instructions and final walk-throughs are harder to schedule when offices are closed.

You can reduce the risk by:

- Getting **preapproved now**, before you write an offer
- Returning lender document requests the same day
- Scheduling the inspection as soon as the contract is signed
- Choosing a closing date that is not the last business hour before a holiday
- Avoiding new credit, as we explain in our [holiday spending and mortgage approval](/posts/holiday-spending-mortgage-approval/) guide

## Is the Market Any Easier for a Year-End Buyer?

Nationally, the market has loosened a bit compared with last fall. On November 12, 2026, the National Association of REALTORS® reported that existing-home sales in October ran at a 3.75 million seasonally adjusted annual rate, down 5.5% from September and 8.8% from a year earlier. Inventory rose to **1.59 million** homes, a **5.1-month supply**, up from 4.4 months in October 2025. The median existing-home price was **$416,700**, up 0.4% year over year.

NAR's seasonal research has also long noted that December through February is typically the slowest stretch of the year, with fewer buyers and more time on market. Less competition does not mean a bargain in every neighborhood, and it does not speed up underwriting. It can mean you have a little more room to negotiate a closing date that actually fits a mortgage.

Freddie Mac's weekly average for a 30-year fixed loan was **7.11%** on November 12, 2026, down from 7.18% the prior week and the lowest reading in seven weeks. A year earlier it was 6.24%. Market averages are not quotes, but a slightly lower rate than last month is useful context if you are deciding whether to start now.
