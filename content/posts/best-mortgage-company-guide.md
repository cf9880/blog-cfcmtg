## What makes the best mortgage company?

There's no official ranking that fits every borrower. A lender that's great for a jumbo buyer may not be the right fit for a first-time FHA buyer or a real estate investor. Instead, judge every mortgage company on the same five things:

1. **Loan options.** Can they offer the loan that fits you: conventional, FHA, VA, USDA, jumbo, or investor loans?
2. **Total cost.** Rate, APR, points, and lender fees, all shown on the official **Loan Estimate**.
3. **Process.** Is applying simple? Can you upload documents online and track progress?
4. **Speed and reliability.** Do they close on time, and do they communicate deadlines clearly?
5. **Service.** Can you reach a real person when you have a question, including when it isn't 9-to-5?

**Loan Estimate (definition):** a standard three-page form lenders must give you within three business days of applying. It makes it easy to compare rate, payment, and closing costs side by side ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/loan-estimate/)).

## How to choose a mortgage company: a 7-step checklist

1. **Check your credit** and know your rough budget before you call.
2. **Get quotes from at least three** lenders or brokers. Freddie Mac research found borrowers who got more quotes could save meaningfully over the life of the loan.
3. **Request quotes the same day** with the same loan amount, down payment, and lock period so they're truly comparable.
4. **Compare the Loan Estimates** line by line: rate, APR, points, Section A origination charges, and cash to close.
5. **Verify licenses** on NMLS Consumer Access for the company and the loan officer.
6. **Read recent reviews** and ask your real estate agent who closes on time.
7. **Pay attention to communication.** How fast they answer you now is a good preview of the rest of the loan.

## Questions to ask a mortgage lender or broker

Fannie Mae and the National Association of REALTORS® both suggest asking questions like these ([Fannie Mae](https://yourhome.fanniemae.com/buy/find-mortgage-lender)):

- Which loan programs fit my credit, income, and down payment?
- What's the rate **and** the APR? Are there points?
- What lender fees will I pay at closing?
- How long is the rate lock, and what happens if rates drop or my closing is delayed?
- How long do preapproval, appraisal, and closing usually take?
- Do you offer down payment assistance options?
- How will you keep me updated, and how do I reach you after hours?
- Who will service my loan after closing?
- **For brokers:** Which lenders do you work with, and how are you paid?

## What should current homeowners know about refinancing?

Rates are higher than a year ago: Freddie Mac's 30-year average was **7.32%** on Oct. 22, 2026, versus **6.19%** a year earlier ([Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms)). So a simple "lower my rate" refinance won't make sense for many homeowners right now. But a refinance can still help if you want to:

- **Drop mortgage insurance** by moving from FHA to conventional once you have enough equity.
- **Switch from an adjustable rate** to a fixed rate before an adjustment.
- **Take cash out** for renovations or to pay off higher-interest debt.
- **Remove a borrower** after a divorce or other life change.

**Break-even point (definition):** your total closing costs divided by your monthly savings. It's how many months it takes for the refinance to pay for itself. If you plan to move before then, a refinance may not be worth it.
