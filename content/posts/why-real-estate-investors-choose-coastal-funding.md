## What Is a DSCR Loan?

A **DSCR loan** is an investment-property mortgage. Instead of underwriting you mainly on W-2s or tax returns, the lender asks whether the property's rent is likely to cover the payment.

**DSCR = monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA**

A DSCR of **1.0** means the rent covers the payment. A DSCR of **1.25** means the rent is 25% higher than the payment. Many lenders look for 1.0 or higher and price the loan more favorably at higher ratios. Some programs allow a ratio below 1.0 with a larger down payment or a higher credit score. Our [plain-English DSCR guide](/posts/dscr-loan-guide/) shows the math with examples.

These loans are generally **non-QM**, meaning they follow the investor lender's guidelines rather than standard qualified-mortgage rules used on most owner-occupied loans.

## What Terms Do DSCR Lenders Typically Look For?

The figures below are **typical industry ranges**, not Coastal Funding program terms and not a quote:

- **Credit score:** often about 620 to 700 or higher, with better leverage at higher scores
- **Down payment:** commonly 20% to 25% on a purchase (75% to 80% LTV)
- **DSCR:** 1.0 or higher is a common target; some lenders allow lower ratios with tighter terms
- **Reserves:** often 3 to 6 months of payments, sometimes more on larger portfolios
- **Property types:** single-family homes, townhomes, 2- to 4-unit properties, and many condos
- **Documentation:** typically credit, assets, a purchase contract or refinance package, and rent support such as a lease or an appraiser's rent schedule

Lenders still check credit, assets, and the property. A DSCR loan is not a no-review loan. It simply changes the main qualifying question from "How much do you earn?" to "Does this property pay for itself?"

## Can Investors Use a HELOC or Refinance Instead?

Sometimes. If you have equity in a primary home or another rental, a cash-out refinance or a HELOC can fund a down payment on the next property. Coastal Funding does offer HELOCs. Whether that path is better than a standalone DSCR purchase depends on rates, prepayment terms, and how much equity you want to leave in the first property.

If you already have a rental, a DSCR cash-out refinance is another way some investors pull equity for repairs or the next purchase. Compare the new payment and any prepayment penalty before you decide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do real estate investors use DSCR loans?

A DSCR loan qualifies mainly on the property's rental income compared with its monthly payment, not on the investor's personal debt-to-income ratio. That can help self-employed buyers and investors who already hold several rentals.

### What down payment do DSCR loans typically require?

Many DSCR purchases require about 20% to 25% down, or a 75% to 80% loan-to-value. Lower credit scores, condos, or a DSCR below 1.0 often need a larger down payment. Terms vary by lender.

### Can I buy a rental in an LLC with a DSCR loan?

Often, yes. Many DSCR programs allow the loan to be in an LLC or other entity, and lenders usually still require a personal guarantee. Requirements vary by lender.

### Is a DSCR loan the same as a conventional investment loan?
